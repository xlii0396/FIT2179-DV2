Coffee Culture in Melbourne vs Other Australian Cities

This visualisation explores whether Melbourne's reputation as a coffee city is supported by data. It compares coffee prices, population patterns, café concentration, and business distribution across Melbourne and other major Australian cities.

Section 1 — City-Level Context

Before focusing on Melbourne, this section compares major Australian cities. It shows how coffee prices and population size differ across cities, and how these cities have grown over time.

F1 — Average Coffee Price by City

This chart compares the average price of a regular cappuccino across five major Australian cities. It shows that Melbourne is not the most expensive coffee city, even though it is often associated with coffee culture.

F2 — Population of Major Australian Cities

This chart compares the 2025 population of major Australian cities. Sydney and Melbourne have the largest populations, which helps explain why both cities have large café markets.

F3 — Population Growth Trend

This line chart shows population growth from 2001 to 2025. Melbourne and Sydney remain the two largest cities, while Brisbane and Perth also show steady long-term growth.

Section 2 — Melbourne Coffee Landscape

This section focuses on Melbourne itself. It shows where cafés are concentrated and how the café landscape is shaped by different areas and business types.

F4 — Café Distribution Across Melbourne Areas

This chart ranks Melbourne areas by the number of cafés. The CBD clearly dominates, showing that Melbourne's coffee activity is highly concentrated in central areas.

F5 — Café Share by Melbourne Area

This donut chart shows the share of cafés by area. It highlights how much of Melbourne's café culture is concentrated in a small number of central locations.

F6 — Business Category Breakdown

This bubble chart shows the major business categories in the Melbourne hospitality dataset. Cafés and restaurants form the largest group, showing the importance of food and drink businesses in Melbourne's urban life.

Section 3 — Relationships and Interactivity

This section connects different variables together. Instead of only showing separate rankings, it explores how population, coffee prices, and city growth relate to each other.

F7 — Population vs Coffee Price

This scatter plot compares city population with average coffee price. It shows that a larger population does not automatically mean higher coffee prices. For example, Sydney has the largest population but one of the lower coffee prices.

F8 — Interactive Population Growth

This interactive line chart allows users to select a city and focus on its population growth. It helps users compare Melbourne's growth against other major Australian cities more clearly.
Click a line or legend item to highlight one city.

Section 4 — Final Understanding

The final section summarises the main story. Melbourne's coffee culture is not only about price or population, but also about café density, central concentration, and the structure of local hospitality businesses.

F9 — Melbourne Coffee Culture Ecosystem

This custom diagram summarises the key factors behind Melbourne's coffee culture: population growth, coffee price, café density, CBD concentration, and business mix. It acts as a bridge between the statistical charts and the final map.

F10 — Café Concentration Map

This choropleth map shows where cafés are concentrated across Melbourne CLUE areas. Darker areas have more cafés, making it clear that Melbourne's café culture is spatially clustered around central districts.

Conclusion

Overall, the data suggests that Melbourne's coffee culture is not simply explained by high prices or population size. Instead, it is shaped by a strong concentration of cafés in central areas, a large hospitality sector, and long-term urban growth.
